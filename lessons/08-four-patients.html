Four Patients

Every number in the earlier units came out of a simulation you watched. Here there are four patients, and you do the arithmetic. Type each value, check it, and see the mean of your four numbers equal the one-step correction. Then fit ε by hand on the same four patients, and finish with what you would report: the TMLE, a standard error and a 95% interval, all from these four rows.

Part 1

The influence function of E[Y(1)], one patient at a time

The target is ψ = E[μ1(X)], the mean outcome if everyone were treated. The initial fit gives μ̂1(x) for each patient and a propensity π(x). The influence function at the fit has two pieces: the X-piece μ̂1(Xi) − ψ̂, which is filled in for you and sums to zero, and the Y-piece Hi·(Yi − μ̂1(Xi)) with Hi = Ai/π(Xi), which you compute. Untreated patients have H = 0: they say nothing about μ1. Then average the Y-pieces. That average is the one-step correction, and ψ̂ plus it is the one-step estimate.

D̂(Zᵢ) = [μ̂₁(Xᵢ) − ψ̂] + Hᵢ (Yᵢ − μ̂₁(Xᵢ)), Hᵢ = Aᵢ / π(Xᵢ), ψ̂ = mean of μ̂₁(Xᵢ)
one-step = ψ̂ + (1/n) Σᵢ Hᵢ (Yᵢ − μ̂₁(Xᵢ))

In one sentenceEach treated patient contributes its residual scaled by 1/π, each untreated patient contributes nothing to the outcome piece, and the average of those contributions is exactly the correction the one-step adds to the plug-in.
Part 2

ε̂ by hand: the same four patients

Now the TMLE step, on the same four patients. Fluctuate the treated-outcome curve along the clever covariate, με(x) = μ̂1(x) + ε·H, and pick ε by least squares over the patients whose outcome μ₁ describes: ε̂ = ΣHiri / ΣHi². Untreated patients have H = 0, so their rows add nothing to either sum (type 0 for them). Compute the two sums, divide, and then the shift each stratum's curve gets, ε̂·H(1, x): 1.25 for young and 4 for old.

ε̂ = Σ Hᵢ rᵢ / Σ Hᵢ² shift of stratum s = ε̂ · 1/π(s) check: Σ Hᵢ (rᵢ − ε̂ Hᵢ) = 0
One treated young patient and one treated old patient. Whose residual pulls ε̂ harder?

In one sentenceε̂ is the no-intercept least-squares slope of the residuals on the clever covariate. The old patient's residual enters the numerator with weight 4 against 1.25, which is the reweighting that gives every stratum evidence in proportion to its share of ψ.

Part 3

The payoff: TMLE, a standard error, and an interval

You now have everything for a reported result, from the same four patients. First finish the TMLE: the updated curve is μ*₁(x) = μ̂₁(x) + ε̂/π(x) at every patient's covariate, treated or not, and ψTMLE is its mean over the four. Then the uncertainty: the influence-function values D̂(Zᵢ) you built in Part 1 (X-piece plus Y-piece) have a spread, and the standard error of the one-step is sd(D̂)/√n.

ψ_TMLE = (1/n) Σᵢ μ*₁(Xᵢ) D̂ᵢ = [μ̂₁(Xᵢ) − ψ̂] + Hᵢ(Yᵢ − μ̂₁(Xᵢ)) SE = sd(D̂)/√n 95% interval: one-step ± 1.96 · SE

In one sentenceThe same four numbers D̂(Zᵢ) do three jobs: their mean is the correction, their spread gives the standard error, and TMLE, which solves Pₙ D = 0 by moving the curve instead of adding the mean, lands on a different number here because four patients and propensities that do not match the observed treated fractions make the second-order difference visible.

Honesty about n = 4: the arithmetic is exact, but a normal interval from four patients is not trustworthy (the sd is itself very noisy, and a t-quantile with 3 degrees of freedom would be 3.18, not 1.96). The standard errors lesson shows when sd(D̂)/√n earns its coverage. What is exact here: every number on this page. What is schematic: the four patients and their propensities, chosen to keep the arithmetic hand-sized.
