Causality · a question you can hold in your hands

Whose treatment changes whose outcome?

Eight connected people. Toggle one treatment and predict whose outcome moves. Then see which of three different effects each randomization design can estimate, exactly, from enumeration of every assignment.

In the roadmap every estimand assumed that one person's treatment leaves everyone else's outcome alone, and in the inference lab the randomized contrast estimated that one effect. Here that assumption is dropped on purpose: when treatments spill over a network, the same contrast estimates a different quantity, and which quantities can be estimated at all depends on what you randomize.

Further reading: Aronow & Samii: Estimating average causal effects under general interference; Hudgens & Halloran: Toward causal inference with interference. The eight-unit network, the linear potential-outcome rule and the exposure summary g are a teaching model; every number on this page is computed by enumeration in the accompanying kernel under the assumptions stated beside each step.
